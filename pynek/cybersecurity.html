The practice

Testing that ends in a fix, not a finding count

A report that lists two hundred issues without saying which three matter this quarter has moved the problem rather than solved it. We rate findings against the system they sit on and the data that system holds, and the remediation section names the change to make rather than the control family to consider.

Everything is authorised in writing before it starts. Exploitation proves reachability and impact — we do not exfiltrate live customer data, pivot past agreed boundaries or leave anything behind on your systems.

Where a finding needs a design change rather than a patch, we say so plainly, and we will tell you when a test would not tell you anything you do not already know.

Questions

What clients ask first

How often should we be testing?

Annual penetration testing with quarterly vulnerability assessment is a reasonable baseline for most organisations, plus a test whenever something material changes — a new application, a migration, an acquisition or a significant architecture change. Testing on a fixed calendar and never in response to change is how a clean report from March coexists with an exposed system from June.

Can you test if we are entirely on cloud infrastructure?

Yes. Cloud changes what is yours to test rather than whether testing is possible. Your applications, configuration, identity setup and data are in scope; the provider’s underlying platform is not. Major providers permit testing of your own resources, and some require notification for particular services — we confirm which rules apply during scoping.

Do you provide a certificate or attestation we can share?

We provide a summary letter confirming the engagement, its scope, the dates and the closure status of findings after retest. That is what auditors and enterprise customers normally ask for. We do not issue a pass mark, and we will not describe a system as secure — a test states what was found in a defined scope at a point in time, and anything stronger than that would be misleading.

Tell us the system, the timeline and what you are worried about. We will tell you what the engagement would actually involve — or that you do not need one.
