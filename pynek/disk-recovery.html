Disk Recovery

Getting data back from drives that have failed, been damaged, or stopped mounting — handled to forensic standards.

Digital Forensics

Overview

A failed drive is often the only copy of something that matters — a case file, an accounts system, a project nobody backed up. We work on hard disks, SSDs, USB media and memory cards that have stopped responding, been formatted, been physically damaged, or simply started returning errors.

Because this sits inside our forensics practice, every case is handled the same way as evidence: the drive is assessed before anything is written to it, work is performed on a forensic image rather than the original wherever possible, and the process is documented. That matters whether the recovery is a business continuity problem or something that may later be examined.

We assess first and tell you honestly what is recoverable, what is not, and what it will take — before you commit. Drives that have been opened outside a cleanroom, or repeatedly powered on after failure, are considerably harder to recover, so the sooner the drive stops being used, the better the outcome.

What you get

  • Free initial assessment and honest feasibility opinion
  • Logical recovery — deleted, formatted and corrupt volumes
  • Firmware-level repair for drives with service-area faults
  • Physical recovery, including cleanroom work where required
  • Encrypted volume recovery where you hold the credentials
  • Recovered data returned on fresh media, with a file listing

Coverage

What we recover from

Scope one media type or a whole set — the assessment and handling discipline stay the same.

Logical failures

Deleted or formatted volumes, corrupt partition tables and file systems, failed operating-system upgrades, and accidental overwrites where the underlying data still survives.

Firmware & electronic faults

Drives that spin but never become ready, are detected with the wrong capacity, or fail in the service area — repaired at module level on professional recovery hardware.

Physical damage

Head failures, seized motors, and drives damaged by impact, water or fire. Invasive work is performed in controlled conditions, imaging in passes so the most valuable areas are read first.

SSD & flash media

Solid-state drives, USB sticks and memory cards, including controller-level work where the flash itself is still readable.

Encrypted volumes

BitLocker and comparable full-disk encryption, recovered where you can supply the recovery key, password or escrow record — we do not defeat encryption you have no right to open.

RAID & multi-disk sets

Arrays and NAS units where one or more members have failed, rebuilt from images of the surviving disks rather than by re-running a risky in-place rebuild.

Stop using the drive and call us

Every additional power-on can reduce what is recoverable. Tell us what happened and we’ll advise before anything is attempted.
