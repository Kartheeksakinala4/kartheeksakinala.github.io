Data off drives that no longer mount, spin or respond

Logical and physical recovery from failed hard drives, SSDs, RAID sets and flash media — including firmware-level work on drives that a normal machine will not even detect.

Disk Recovery

Every additional power-on is a chance to lose it permanently

A drive that clicks, a RAID that will not rebuild, an SSD that has dropped to read-only, a phone card that reports zero bytes — each has a different failure mode, and the wrong first move makes several of them unrecoverable. Repeatedly power-cycling a drive with a failing head assembly, in particular, is how a recoverable fault becomes scored platters.

We start with a non-destructive assessment: what has failed, whether the data is likely recoverable, and what it would take. You get that answer, with an honest likelihood, before any recovery fee is committed.

Recovery work runs from logical repair — rebuilding partition tables, file systems and RAID parameters — through firmware-level intervention on the drive's service area, up to head and platter work carried out in a controlled clean environment with donor components. Where the matter may become evidential, recovery is performed to forensic standards so the result is still usable in proceedings.

Coverage

What we recover from

Mechanical hard drives

Clicking, grinding or silent drives — head assembly replacement, motor seizure, stuck heads and platter damage assessment using matched donors.

Solid state drives

Controller failure, firmware corruption, read-only lockout after wear thresholds, and sudden capacity loss on NVMe and SATA SSDs.

RAID and NAS

Failed rebuilds, multiple member failures, lost or corrupted array configuration, and parameter reconstruction where the controller metadata is gone.

Firmware and service area

Drives the BIOS cannot see, drives reporting zero capacity, and service area corruption that makes a mechanically sound disk unreadable.

Logical corruption

Deleted partitions, reformatted volumes, corrupted file systems, failed operating system upgrades and ransomware-damaged structures.

Flash and removable media

USB sticks, SD and microSD cards, and camera media with controller failure, physical damage or corrupted directory structures.

Questions

What clients ask first

What should we do right now, before sending it?

Stop using the device and leave it powered off. Do not run recovery utilities against a drive making unusual noise — they force repeated reads that accelerate physical damage. Do not open the drive. Do not freeze it. If a RAID has failed, do not initiate another rebuild: a failed rebuild frequently destroys what a recovery would otherwise have retrieved. Note what happened and in what order, and call us.

Can you guarantee the data will come back?

No, and nobody honest will. Platters that have been scored by a crashed head, NAND cells that have lost charge, and data that has been genuinely overwritten are gone. That is exactly why the assessment comes first — you get a realistic likelihood before committing to a recovery fee, rather than an optimistic one afterwards.

How is the evidence handled once it reaches you?

It is logged on receipt with photographs, serial numbers and visible condition, stored in a secured area, and entered on a custody record that captures every subsequent transfer with date, time and signature. Examination is performed on a verified working copy — the original is not analysed directly. The original is returned to you or held under instruction, and the custody record forms part of the report.
