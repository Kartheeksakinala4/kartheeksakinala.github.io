E-mail Forensics

The header carries the part nobody forges convincingly

Two questions come up constantly. The first is a payment diverted by a message that appeared to come from a supplier or a director. The second is a dispute in which one side produces an e-mail and the other says it was never sent, or not in that form.

Both are answered from the transport record rather than the message body. Received headers document each hop with timestamps and server identities; SPF, DKIM and DMARC results record whether the sending infrastructure was authorised and whether the signed content was altered in transit; message identifiers, boundaries and encoding artefacts reveal which client actually generated the message.

For compromise cases we go further into the mailbox: audit and sign-in logs, inbox rules created to hide replies, forwarding rules to external addresses, delegate permissions, and OAuth application grants — which is how access commonly persists after a password reset.

1–2 days for message authentication; 3–5 for a compromise investigation

Coverage

What the examination establishes

Header and transport chain

Every hop the message passed through, with server identities and timestamps — and whether that chain is internally consistent or has been fabricated.

Authentication results

SPF, DKIM and DMARC evaluation: whether the sending server was authorised for the domain, and whether signed content was modified after signing.

Spoofing and impersonation

Display-name spoofing, lookalike sending domains, reply-to divergence, and thread hijacking where an attacker replies into a genuine existing conversation.

Account compromise

Sign-in logs, impossible-travel indicators, malicious inbox rules, external forwarding, delegate access and OAuth grants that survive a password change.

Message authentication

Whether a disputed message is consistent with its claimed origin, client and date, or shows signs of editing, backdating or export-and-reimport.

Attachments and links

Attachment analysis, embedded macro and script examination, and redirect chain tracing for links, in a contained environment.

Questions

What clients ask first

We only have a forwarded copy or a screenshot. Is that enough?

Not for authentication. Forwarding rewrites the header chain and a screenshot carries none of it, so neither can establish origin. What we need is the original message exported as .eml or .msg from the mailbox that received it. If the original has been deleted, the mail platform’s message trace logs may still hold enough to work with — but only inside the retention window, which is why this should not wait.

Money has already left. Is forensics still worth it?

Yes, on two counts. Speed matters for recall: the faster the receiving bank has a documented account of the fraud, the better the prospect of a hold on the funds. And the investigation establishes whether the compromise was on your side or your counterparty’s, which determines liability, what you must tell your insurer, and whether an attacker still has access to your mailbox.

Will this stand up in court?

The examination is conducted so that it can. Acquisition is write-blocked and hash-verified, custody is documented from receipt to return, the method is recorded in enough detail to be repeated by another examiner, and the report carries a named examiner’s statement. Where an electronic record is to be produced, we supply the certificate required under Section 63 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 (previously Section 65B of the Indian Evidence Act, 1872). Admissibility is ultimately a matter for the court, and your counsel should review the material before it is filed.
