Storage Media Forensics

Media carries history the file system does not show

A file system view shows you what the operating system currently believes exists. A media-level examination shows you what is physically present: unallocated space holding fragments from a previous format, host protected areas and device configuration overlays hiding capacity from the operating system, partition gaps, and slack space at the end of every allocated cluster.

This is the discipline for questions about the medium itself — whether a USB device was used to carry specific files, what a card held before it was reformatted in a camera, whether a drive has been wiped and how thoroughly, or whether declared capacity matches physical capacity.

Imaging is performed through a write blocker and verified by hash. Where a device is failing, acquisition is done with read-retry parameters tuned to the fault so we get one good pass rather than repeatedly stressing a dying drive.

Coverage

What a media examination covers

Full physical imaging

Sector-level acquisition of the entire device including areas the operating system never presents, verified by hash against the source.

Hidden and reserved areas

Host Protected Area and Device Configuration Overlay detection — capacity deliberately hidden from the operating system, and what is stored in it.

File carving

Recovery of documents, images, archives and databases from unallocated space by signature, independent of any surviving directory entry.

Prior use and formatting

Evidence of earlier file systems, previous partition schemes and residual data surviving a reformat — what the medium held before its current life.

Wiping analysis

Whether the media was deliberately wiped, by what class of tool, how completely, and when — frequently the material fact in an employment matter.

Encryption and containers

Detection of full-disk encryption, hidden volumes and encrypted containers, with decryption where keys or credentials are lawfully available.

Questions

What clients ask first

Will this stand up in court?

The examination is conducted so that it can. Acquisition is write-blocked and hash-verified, custody is documented from receipt to return, the method is recorded in enough detail to be repeated by another examiner, and the report carries a named examiner’s statement. Where an electronic record is to be produced, we supply the certificate required under Section 63 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 (previously Section 65B of the Indian Evidence Act, 1872). Admissibility is ultimately a matter for the court, and your counsel should review the material before it is filed.

Can you recover data from an SSD that was deleted?

Sometimes, and materially less often than from a mechanical drive. Most SSDs run TRIM, which allows the controller to erase blocks belonging to deleted files during background garbage collection — frequently within minutes and without the operating system being involved. Fragments may still be recoverable from over-provisioned areas or where TRIM was disabled, but we will not promise mechanical-drive results on flash.

How is the evidence handled once it reaches you?

It is logged on receipt with photographs, serial numbers and visible condition, stored in a secured area, and entered on a custody record that captures every subsequent transfer with date, time and signature. Examination is performed on a verified working copy — the original is not analysed directly. The original is returned to you or held under instruction, and the custody record forms part of the report.
