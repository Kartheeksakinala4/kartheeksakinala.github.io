How we teach

Configure it, break it, then detect the break

The shortest route from a concept to a skill is consequence. Every module introduces something, makes the participant build or run it, then makes them attack or investigate what they just produced — because a control you have never bypassed is a control you do not really understand.

Every participant gets their own lab environment, kept for the duration of the program and rebuilt as the syllabus progresses. Case material is drawn from anonymised engagements, so the scenarios are how incidents actually begin rather than how a textbook says they do.

Our trainers are practitioners on live engagements. That constrains how many cohorts we run, and it is the reason the material stays current.

Questions

What clients ask first

Which program should someone start with?

For an administrator or network engineer, Network Security — it builds on what they already know and gives the fastest practical return. For someone entering the field generally, Cybersecurity. For investigators, audit staff or legal professionals, Digital Forensics, which assumes no security background. If a candidate is unsure, we will talk to them before taking an enrolment.

Can you train our whole team on site?

Yes. Closed cohorts run at your premises or in a private online classroom, with content adapted to your operating systems, network design and tooling — and to your own anonymised incidents as case material if you want them used. Minimum cohort size and schedule are agreed during scoping.

Is there placement assistance?

We do not run a placement programme and we will not claim one. What the programs produce is demonstrable work — a lab build, written detections, an incident report, an examiner’s statement defended under challenge — which is materially more useful in an interview than a certificate alone.
